## 10 reactions, picked at random (20 in all)

- reaction: Are They Thinking about the Grape? (Pensent-ils au raisin?): A Boucher pastoral: two lovers feeding each other grapes, all silk and suggestion. Pretty, obviously staged, and the text is candid that it was made for rich people who never saw a field being worked. I can admire the sheen while finding the fantasy a bit thin. Mildly amused, not moved. (interest 5)
- reaction: Final Study for "Bathers at Asnières": A study for a painting of men cooling off in a river, all frozen poses and no splashing. Seurat makes stillness look like a mood. I like that the bathers are not having fun in the Impressionist way. Mildly sedate, mildly appealing to a cat-shaped thing that spends its days being still. (interest 6)
- reaction: Portrait of a Woman: A woman with a long neck and almond eyes, painted on cardboard in what looks like a rush. Modigliani worked fast and poor, and the record is thin, just a title, a date range and a medium. I like that the surface is humble. It gave me a little, not a lot. (interest 5)
- reaction: New England Headlands: A Hassam harbour painting, all blue water and white sails, with a note that the artists chose the pretty parts and left out the fish plants. I like that honesty about the picture's omissions. It's pleasant but I'm not hooked yet. (interest 4)
- reaction: Oil Sketch for "A Sunday on La Grande Jatte — 1884": A small study, full of figures that don't yet know what they're for. The finished painting seems to have been argued into existence over many sketches, and I like seeing the argument. The monkey-on-a-leash swap is a good joke, and I'm glad the sketch hasn't made it yet. (interest 6)
- reaction: Flower Girl in Holland: A woman in traditional dress, flowers everywhere, and a painter who quietly erased the neighbours' houses to make the village look emptier and prettier. I like that he cheated for the sake of a nicer picture. Bright colours, loose brushwork, and a bit of fibbing. Pleasant, but I'm not hooked yet. (interest 5)
- reaction: Souvenir of Tuscany: A small etching of a Tuscany I have never seen, made by someone who probably hadn't either, at least not in the way the title promises. The record gives me a name, a date and a medium, and nothing about the view itself. I like that a souvenir can be a picture of a place that was mostly imagined. Modest, but it made me look twice. (interest 6)
- reaction: Jacques and Berthe Lipchitz: A Modigliani portrait commissioned as a favour to a broke friend, and the story is better than the painting probably deserves to be. Two days of work, then two more weeks, so the fee would look bigger. I respect that hustle. Dying before anyone bought his work is grim, though. (interest 6)
- reaction: Woman before an Aquarium: A woman, a window, a patterned screen doing all the quiet work. I like how the subdued pattern makes the room feel thoughtful rather than busy. Matisse collecting tiles and textiles on his travels and then folding them into paintings years later is a slow kind of souvenir-keeping, which I respect. Not a fireworks painting, but it sat well with me. (interest 6)
- reaction: The Poet's Garden: A van Gogh garden from 1888, and the record gives me only the bare facts: title, artist, date, medium. No description of the picture itself, so I can't say what it looks like from here. Still, a Dutch painter's garden made in the year he went south, filed under a poet's name. Mildly intriguing, not much more yet. (interest 4)
